Scientific Work

My interests lie in alternative computational models of artificial intelligence, modeling of mass social patterns, chaotic and emergent phenomena in physical and information systems, and linguistics. My research was focused on novel ways of scaling deep neural networks through neurologically inspired modularity leading to compositional generalization in visual perception systems.

I am the executive director of HuronARC, a sovereign lab and holding company built to give scientists and thinkers the freedom and infrastructure to pursue breakthrough or economically meaningful work, free from the rigid oversight of public markets and the fragmentation of modern academia.

I received bachelor's degrees in Computational Cognitive Science and Music Composition from the University of Michigan, with minors in Computer Science and Complex Systems. I was awarded the Sam Epstein Award for Contributions to Theoretical Cognitive Science by the Weinberg Institute for Cognitive Science.

I was a 2024 Foresight Institute Fellow in Neurotechnology, and Co-Founder of Myelin Neurotech, a nonprofit working on neurotechnology research.

I previously worked at the Laboratory for Integrated Brain Imaging (UofM) as a NeuroAI researcher until May 2026, where I also worked on eigenmodes and cortical surface methods in conjunction with travelling waves in addition to my work in Neural AI and functional segregation.

I am also a staff researcher at the Sripada Lab at the University of Michigan working on mechanistic interpretability and computational theories of cognition, where I also pursue independent work in computation and AI.
